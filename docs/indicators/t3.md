# Tillson T3 (`t3`)

Three passes of a "generalised DEMA" built from one EMA length, i.e. six cascaded EMAs combined with fixed
coefficients. Use it when you want a very smooth line with less [lag](glossary:lag) than a plain EMA cascade and only mild overshoot.

## What it measures
Where the trend is now, if you want a line almost as smooth as a long chain of EMAs but only a bar or two behind
price. At the default it follows price with about 1.8 bars of lag and very little noise; its turns are smooth and come
a bar or two after price turns.

Move the length and watch the lag and the smoothness change together (the lag grows linearly with the length):

## Formula

With $E$ the $\mathrm{EMA}_n$ operator and the volume factor $v \in [0, 1]$:

$$
GD(x) = (1+v)\,E(x) - v\,E(E(x)), \qquad \textcolor{#5C6BC0}{T3} = GD(GD(GD(x))).
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical range |
|---|---|---|
| $x$ | the source series (close by default) | price |
| $E$ | the $\mathrm{EMA}_n$ operator, $\alpha = \frac{2}{n+1}$ (SMA-seeded) | linear filter |
| $n$ | the length of each of the six EMAs | bars, default 5 |
| $v$ | the volume factor (a lag-compensation weight, not volume) | 0–1, default 0.7 |
| $GD$ | one "generalised DEMA" pass | price |
| $\textcolor{#5C6BC0}{T3}$ | the indicator: three $GD$ passes | price |

All operators are linear and commute, so $T3 = \big((1+v)E - vE^2\big)^3$ expands by the binomial theorem over the
cascade $e_k = E^k(x)$:

$$
\textcolor{#5C6BC0}{T3} = c_1 e_6 + c_2 e_5 + c_3 e_4 + c_4 e_3, \qquad
\begin{aligned}
c_1 &= -v^3, & c_2 &= 3v^2 + 3v^3, \\
c_3 &= -6v^2 - 3v - 3v^3, & c_4 &= 1 + 3v + 3v^2 + v^3 = (1+v)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical range |
|---|---|---|
| $e_k$ | $E$ applied $k$ times to $x$ ($e_3 \dots e_6$ are used) | price |
| $c_1 \dots c_4$ | fixed weights from $v$; they sum to 1 | $-6.07$ to $4.91$ at $v = 0.7$ |

At $v = 0.7$: $c_1 = -0.343$, $c_2 = 2.499$, $c_3 = -6.069$, $c_4 = 4.913$; they sum to $\big((1+v) - v\big)^3 = 1$
(unit DC gain). In $z$: $E(z) = \frac{\alpha}{1 - (1-\alpha)z^{-1}}$, $\alpha = \frac{2}{n+1}$, and
$H(z) = E(z)^3\big((1+v) - vE(z)\big)^3$.

## Kernel, lag and noise

T3 is linear and time-invariant: an [IIR filter](glossary:iir-filter) with a sixth-order pole at $z = 1 - \alpha$.

**Lag.** The lag of a unit-gain filter is its first moment $\bar k = \sum_k k\,h_k$. Two rules follow from
$\bar k = -\frac{d}{d\omega}\arg H\big|_{\omega=0}$: lags **add** in a cascade, and a unit-gain **linear combination**
$aH_1 + (1-a)H_2$ has lag $aL_1 + (1-a)L_2$ (weights may be negative). With $L_E = \frac{n-1}{2}$:

$$
L_{GD} = (1+v)L_E - v\,(2L_E) = (1-v)\,L_E, \qquad \textcolor{#5C6BC0}{L_{T3}} = 3(1-v)\,\frac{n-1}{2}.
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical range |
|---|---|---|
| $L_E$ | lag of one $\mathrm{EMA}_n$, $\frac{n-1}{2}$ | bars (2 at $n = 5$) |
| $L_{GD}$ | lag of one $GD$ pass; $E^2$ has lag $2L_E$ | bars |
| $\textcolor{#5C6BC0}{L_{T3}}$ | lag of T3: three $GD$ passes in cascade | bars (1.8 at the default) |

At $n = 5$, $v = 0.7$: $L_{T3} = 3 \cdot 0.3 \cdot 2 = 1.8$ bars (measured 1.800).

**Limits.** $v = 0$: $GD = E$ and $T3 = E^3$, the plain triple EMA cascade (lag $3\frac{n-1}{2} = 6$ at $n = 5$; not
TEMA). $v = 1$: $GD = 2E - E^2 = \mathrm{DEMA}$ and $T3 = \mathrm{DEMA}^3$, lag 0, with the largest overshoot.

**Noise and negative weights.** The NRF $\sum h_k^2$ has no simple closed form; measured at $n = 5$:

| $v$ | Lag (bars) | NRF | Step overshoot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 6.00 | 0.0771 | none |
| 0.5 | 3.00 | 0.1337 | 2.9 % |
| 0.7 (default) | 1.80 | 0.1773 | 9.2 % |
| 1 | 0.00 | 0.2819 | 25.0 % |

At the default the weights are negative from $k = 9$ on (minimum $-0.0133$ at $k = 12$, total negative weight
$-0.093$): small, which is why the overshoot stays under 10 %. For comparison, an EMA with the same 1.8-bar lag
($n = 4.6$) has NRF $1/4.6 = 0.217$: T3 is about 18 % quieter at equal lag.

Move the volume factor on a series with a $-15$ % [gap](glossary:gap): at 0 the line glides into the gap, near 1 it catches up fast
and overshoots below the new level before settling.

## Warm-up
First valid bar index: $6(n - 1)$, i.e. 24 at the default. Each EMA seeds with the SMA of its first $n$ valid inputs,
so every stage adds $n - 1$ bars. The chart loads $\max(50n, 250)$ bars of extra history.

## Filter view
The drawer shows the measured impulse, step, magnitude and [group delay](glossary:group-delay) of the one output line:

- **Impulse**: starts at $h_0 = 0.117$, peaks at $k = 2$ (0.216), dips slightly negative from $k = 9$.
- **Step**: rises past 1 at bar 6 and peaks at 1.092 at bar 8 (9.2 % overshoot), then settles at the DC gain 1.
- **$\lvert H\rvert$ in dB vs period** (log axis, 2 to 200 bars): a gentle bump of $+0.52$ dB near $P \approx 35$
  bars (the price of the negative weights), $-3$ dB at $P \approx 13.2$, about $-18$ dB at $P = 5$ and $-30.4$ dB at
  $P = 2$. No zeros: the stopband falls smoothly.
- **Group delay**: 1.8 bars at long periods, rising to a maximum of 3.36 bars near $P \approx 26$, and negative
  below $P \approx 6.3$ ($-1.09$ at $P = 2$). The negative delay only affects cycles already attenuated by more than
  18 dB.

## How to read it
- **Price above / below the line.** On a steady trend of slope $b$ per bar the line runs $L_{T3}\,b$ behind price
  (1.8 $b$ at the default), so in a clean uptrend price stays just above it. Because the lag is so short, price also
  crosses the default T3 on almost every pullback: a close below T3 5 says little more than "the last two bars were
  down".
- **Slope.** The slope of T3 is a smoothed slope of price, delayed by about $L_{T3}$ bars. Thanks to the low NRF the
  slope changes sign rarely, so a turn of the line is a cleaner trend-change mark than a price cross.
- **Distance from price.** After a jump the line overshoots by up to 9.2 % of the jump at the default (25 % at
  $v = 1$): a short stretch where price sits on the "wrong" side of T3 right after a gap is the overshoot, not a new
  trend.
- **Trend vs range.** In a trend the line is smooth and on one side of price; in a range it is flat and price crosses
  it constantly.
- **The parameters.** The length scales the lag linearly ($3(1-v)\frac{n-1}{2}$) and lowers the noise; the volume
  factor trades lag for overshoot at a fixed length. A longer T3 (e.g. 20, lag 8.55 bars) is the one whose side of
  price carries information (next section).

## Why it works
Write the price as the sum of its bar returns, $x_t - x_{t-k} = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} r_{t-j}$. For any unit-gain [kernel](glossary:kernel)
$h$, $x_t - y_t = \sum_k h_k (x_t - x_{t-k})$, and swapping the sums (derivation on the Moving Average page):

$$
x_t - \textcolor{#5C6BC0}{T3_t} = \sum_{j \ge 0} w_j\, r_{t-j}, \qquad w_j = \sum_{k > j} h_k, \qquad \sum_{j \ge 0} w_j = \textcolor{#5C6BC0}{L_{T3}}.
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical range |
|---|---|---|
| $x_t$ | the price (log price for returns) at bar $t$ | price |
| $\textcolor{#5C6BC0}{T3_t}$ | the T3 value at bar $t$ | price |
| $r_{t-j}$ | the return of bar $t - j$ | about $\pm 1$–2 % per bar |
| $h_k$ | the T3 kernel (impulse response), $\sum_k h_k = 1$ | weights |
| $w_j$ | weight of the return $j$ bars ago (the kernel's tail beyond $j$) | 0 to 1 |
| $\textcolor{#5C6BC0}{L_{T3}}$ | the lag; also the total weight of past returns | bars |

So "close above T3" means a weighted sum of recent returns is positive, and the total weight is the lag: 1.8 bars of
returns at the default, 8.55 at length 20. (The negative kernel tail makes the far weights $w_j$ slightly negative.)
Whether that sum says anything about the next $h$ bars depends on the market.

**[Random walk](glossary:random-walk).** Future returns are independent of past ones: after every bar the expected sum of the next $h$
returns is $h\mu$, so no rule on past prices has an [edge](glossary:edge). The same holds in a steady uptrend, where every bar has the
same expected return.

**Range** ([mean reversion](glossary:mean-reversion) around a level $m$). With $d_t = x_t - m$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[x_{t+h} - x_t \mid d_t] = -\big(1 - (1-\theta)^h\big)\, d_t \approx -0.57\, d_t \quad (\theta = 0.08,\ h = 10)
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical range |
|---|---|---|
| $m$ | the level the range reverts to ($\ln 100$ in the scenario) | log price |
| $d_t$ | deviation of the log price from $m$ | about $\pm 4$ % |
| $\theta$ | the reversion speed per bar | 0.08 in the scenario |
| $h$ | the look-ahead | bars, 10 in the lab |

A close below a slow line (a proxy for $m$) therefore predicts a rise.

**Reversal** (drift $+0.25$ % per bar, then $-0.25$ %). A close below a slow line mostly happens in the falling half,
where the next 10 bars lose about 2.5 % on average, while the average bar of the whole sample earns about 0.

backtest (no costs), sensitive to how many rules you try, and not investment advice.

## Combine with
T3 is a good **trigger** line, not a regime detector. Pair it with:

- a **trend filter** that knows the regime: close above SMA 200 (lag 99.5 bars: a weighted sum of the last 199
  returns);
- a T3 **crossover** as the trigger: T3 5 above T3 20 is the sign of a difference filter with no DC gain, i.e. a
  smoothed slope (see the MA Cross page);
- an **RSI** above 50 as [momentum](glossary:momentum) confirmation.

With seed 1 on the reversal: SMA 200 alone $+1.49$ % ($-0.05$ to $+3.06$, n = 36), T3 5 above T3 20 alone $+0.83$ %
($-0.52$ to $+2.19$, n = 45), RSI above 50 alone $+1.59$ % ($+0.48$ to $+2.70$, n = 51); the filter and the trigger
together $+2.27$ % ($+0.61$ to $+3.95$, n = 26); all three $+2.28$ % ($+0.54$ to $+4.06$, n = 25), so the RSI adds
nothing here. On the range the same pair is $-1.00$ % ($-2.09$ to $+0.11$, n = 40): trend rules lose in a
mean-reverting market. One sample, no costs, not investment advice.

## Pitfalls
- The "volume factor" has nothing to do with volume: it is the lag-compensation weight $v$.
- The length is per EMA: at $n = 5$ the [impulse response](glossary:impulse-response) is visible for about 35 bars (99 % of its absolute weight
  within the first 19) and the [warm-up](glossary:warm-up) is $6(n-1)$.
- Raising $v$ towards 1 removes lag but overshoots up to 25 % (at $n = 5$) and amplifies mid-length cycles: at
  $v = 1$ the $\lvert H\rvert$ bump grows to $+2.27$ dB near $P \approx 24$ bars.
- $v = 0$ is the triple EMA cascade, not TEMA.
